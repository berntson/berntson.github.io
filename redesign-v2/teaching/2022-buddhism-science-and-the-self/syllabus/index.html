Buddhism, Science, and the Self

Spring 2022 · Rutgers University · Philosophy 369

Daniel Berntson · daniel.berntson@rutgers.edu · Office hours: Fridays 12:00–2:00pm (Zoom)

Tu, Th 3:50–5:10pm · 106 Somerset Street, 3rd Floor

Description

This course is an introduction to Buddhist philosophy. As such, we will cover the basic teaching of Buddhism, and you will meet many of the most prominent figures from ancient Buddhist philosophy. The main goal of the course, though, is not merely historical. The main goal is to have a conversation about Buddhism and science. To what extent does science challenge Buddhism? To what extent does science suggest that the basic teachings of Buddhism might be true? And what might the Buddhist philosophical tradition have to teach us about the nature of science and scientific inquiry?

Required Texts

Most readings for the course will be distributed through Canvas. The only required text is listed below, which can be purchased in either physical or electronic form.

  • Siderits, Mark. Buddhism as Philosophy. Second edition. Indianapolis: Hackett Publishing Company, Inc, 2021.

Policies

Participation

You are expected to generally attend class. Participation is based on not only your attendance, but also your active contributions. You should talk in small groups, ask questions, raise objections, and so on.

Talking in class can be easier for some than others. If you find this difficult, do your best, but you can also earn participation credit by attending office hours. Come with questions and ideas!

Journal

For the course journal, you will submit a short entry before each class through Canvas. The entry should be a reflection on the readings for that class period. It should be at least 300 words, but you are also welcome to write more, if you want.

Your journal will be submitted at the end of the semester as a single electronic document. All of your entries will then be graded as a single package. Between your initial submission of an entry and your final submission of the full journal, you are welcome to revise your entries and add content.

Papers

You will be expected to write three short papers over the course of the semester. In these papers, you are expected to show familiarity with the ideas discussed in class. You are also expected to advance your own original philosophical argument. You are free to write on any topic you want, so long as it is clearly relevant to the material that we have covered in class.

Final Exam

The final exam will be held on Canvas. You will be able to take it any time during the final exam period. The questions for your exam will be randomly selected from a large question bank. These questions will include multiple choice questions, short essay questions, and long essay questions. The exam will be cumulative, and so will cover material from the entire course.

Plagiarism

Students often plagiarize because they feel that they have fallen behind or simply cannot do the expected course work. If you find yourself in this position, please talk to me! I want to help. There is no need to cheat to do well in the course, even if you find yourself feeling a bit lost.

If you find yourself unable to complete an assignment by the stated deadline, please contact me. We can work something out and develop a plan for submitting your work. Also, if you are unsure about what counts as plagiarism, you should also contact me. Many cases of plagiarism are accidental, and can be avoided with proper citations.

When students engage in any form of academic dishonesty, I am required to report this to the university.
