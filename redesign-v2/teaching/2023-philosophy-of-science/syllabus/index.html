Philosophy of Science

Spring 2023 · Uppsala University · VT 23

Daniel Berntson · daniel.berntson@filosofi.uu.se

Description

This course is an introduction to the philosophy of science. Rather than aiming to give a systematic overview, our approach will be to cover a selection of topics. Some of these will be about the general nature of scientific inquiry. Does science aim at truth? Is science value neutral? Some will be about the philosophy of physics. What sort of thing is spacetime? What are scientific laws? Others will be about the epistemology and ethics of science. Can science give us reason to believe in things outside our physical universe? And what might sort of unique ethical questions might be raised by new developments in artificial intelligence?

Objectives

  • Gain an understanding of the selected topics in the philosophy of science.
  • Analyze the strengths and weaknesses of the theories studied.
  • Create original counterexamples and counterarguments.
  • Deploy a range of concepts and conceptual tools.
  • Express ideas using clear and concise English prose.

Required Texts

None. All readings will be made available through Studium.

Examinations for the course will consist of a short paper (1000 words) and a final paper (1500 words). Both papers must be written in English.

Policies

Education

The teaching of sessions will not be formally divided into lectures and seminars. Thus, all sessions will be interactive. The goal is for us to do philosophy together by asking questions, raising objections, and having conversations. Slides and lecture notes will regularly be made available. Attendance will not be recorded and is not technically required. But practically speaking, it will be difficult to learn the material and pass the course without regularly attending lectures and participating in discussions.

Plagiarism

Students are responsible for knowing what constitutes cheating and plagiarism, along with the corresponding consequences for engaging in either. Information about cheating and plagiarism can be found on the department website.
